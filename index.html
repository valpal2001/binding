Valerie Hui's Project 3: Binding

Who decides what goes into design history? These readings question the unspoken standards that we have grown to be familiar with. Chou shows how Euro-American modernism pushed East Asian design to the margins. Buzon argues that design thinking keeps whiteness as its default. Holmes-Miller documents why Black designers were missing from the profession. Read together, they ask who gets to set design’s “standard.”

  1. Design History in Globalization and the Place of East Asian Design

    Author
    Wen Huei Chou
    Published
    E&PDE Conference, Salzburg, 2006

    Using Taiwan as a case study, Chou argues that design history treats Euro-American modernism as the standard and pushes East Asian design to the margins.

    EPDE2006TAIWAN

  2. Design Thinking Is a Rebrand for White Supremacy

    Author
    Darin Buzon
    Published
    Medium, 2020

    Buzon argues that design thinking repackages Modernist design, keeps whiteness as its default, and presents itself as a neutral method.

    DTSTANFORD2005USA

  3. Black Designers: Missing in Action

    Author
    Cheryl D. Holmes-Miller
    Published
    Print, September 1987

    Holmes-Miller looks at why so few Black designers worked in graphic design: family pressure, the cost of school, isolation, a lack of mentors, and hidden bias.

    PRINT1987USA
